# Live moves and dance worlds

The Mac and iPhone continuous lobby has two new icon panels: **Dance world** beside the camera controls, and **Mirror and voice** on the left when caring for a Fonster. These preserve the same world renderer, camera and creature rigs. The original gallery and exports remain available.

Dance world offers daylight, a warm solo spotlight, and a disco with an original procedural floor, mirror ball, slow light beams, DJ and speakers. Confetti and balloons are bounded reusable bursts. Daylight/Stop removes the set; the existing Undo restores the previous control state. There is no strobe. Reduce Motion and Still retain a steady party set, suppress beams and falling effects, and use still reaction poses. Pause/background/Low Power stop the shared clock. No soundtrack or paid assets were added.

Mirror and voice starts with both inputs off. Its camera button requests permission only after a deliberate tap. Apple's Vision framework processes face landmarks and confident body joints on a serial utility queue. Only normalized gaze, eye openness, head roll, motion and raised-hand positions reach the UI. Two hand-direction reversals produce a wave with a cooldown. Brief closures mirror blinks; sustained closure settles the friend, reopening wakes it. Missing/invalid detections return to neutral. Motion is smoothed and capped. This is geometric imitation, not emotion, identity or liveness inference. The prototype phone capture is portrait-oriented; landscape camera calibration needs real-device validation.

The microphone requests speech and microphone permission only after its button is tapped. It uses the existing native Speech framework with `requiresOnDeviceRecognition = true`, `supportsOnDeviceRecognition` checks and English commands. It never falls back to a server or installs speech assets. Say one short command: **wave, dance, sleep, jump, blink, spin, stretch, stop**. Final recognition decides the complete phrase. Polite short prefixes are accepted; negation, unknown words and multiple actions are rejected. Audio, camera frames and recognized words are never written to an archive or shared. Fonster sound output is muted while listening to prevent its own chirps from becoming commands. If local speech is unavailable, action icons and typing still work. An interruption turns inputs off; pause/background/Low Power suspends capture. Changing the selected Fonster, returning to the lobby, or Stop turns both inputs off. Opening profile/library/sharing sheets suspends inputs and cancels pending interpretation.

Practice is explicit and reversible: choose a wave, sway or voice rhythm, watch the progress ring, then Keep or Discard. Visual practice requires usable detections; voice rhythm requires three separately recognized dances. Only three bounded movement scalars are saved with the existing local care/personality memory. No body model, accent, voice clone, recording, transcript or private appearance seed is stored. Keep previews the draft, Undo restores the previous style, Reset restores default movement. Existing personality v1 files remain readable because the new field is optional. Account syncing of these learned movement habits remains outside this local prototype.

All controls have hover descriptions and accessibility names. The panel question mark explains each action, permission, interruption and undo. No permission is changed by the build/test tools. Synthetic verification inputs are available only under `--verify-live-inputs` and never open hardware; evidence produced under that flag must be labeled synthetic.

Native API references: [Apple on-device recognition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognitionrequest/requiresondevicerecognition), [Apple body pose detection](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodyposerequest), [Apple Vision pose guidance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/).

Validation and artifact paths are recorded in `evidence/soft-world/live-moves-outcome.json`. Physical camera/eye/wave accuracy and local speech availability are distinct from synthetic routing tests and require opt-in hardware use. Large multi-level clubs and a full band remain future scene additions; the implemented party configuration uses a DJ.
